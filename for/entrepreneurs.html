Focus Supplement for Entrepreneurs: For the Deep-Work Block After a Short Night

By NeuroPouch Team Updated November 2026 ~17 min read
Quick answer

A focus supplement for entrepreneurs can take the edge off a short night so you can start a deep-work block, and it cannot replace the sleep you missed. NeuroPouch Energy & Focus pairs 100 mg caffeine with 150 mg L-theanine, plus Alpha-GPC, Rhodiola and vitamin B12, in a nicotine-free pouch you use up to twice a day. The research behind the ingredients comes from short studies in healthy volunteers, not founders [1].

It's 7:40 and the first deep-work block of the day starts at 8:30. You've had five hours, maybe less, and somewhere in them a child needed you. The proposal that needs two clear hours is open in one tab. The supplier's email, the hiring thread, the bank's question and the customer who wants a call sit in the others. Your head feels as if it was packed in wet cotton overnight.

Oliver knows that morning. He founded NeuroPouch, he's a dad, and he runs several businesses, so short nights come with the job. Some mornings, he says, his watch is "screaming that I slept terribly", and the brain fog is thick enough to write the day off. Then he uses a pouch. Within twenty minutes he feels his brain start firing again "like an old engine turning over on a cold morning", and before he notices he's in deep focus. His own explanation is short: "That's 100 mg caffeine and 150 mg L-Theanine working together."

That is one founder's experience. More than 100 people tried the pouch before launch and reported the same thing, pure focus with no jitters. It was tester feedback, and no clinical trial of the finished product exists, so the rest of this page treats the evidence that way.

Why do short nights wreck the deep-work block you need most?

Deep work needs a stretch where nothing interrupts you and your attention stays on one problem. A short night attacks the first half, because sleepiness doesn't wait for a quiet calendar. In one driving-simulator study, young adults who had slept five hours drove between 2 pm and 5 pm. An 80 mg caffeine energy drink cut their sleep-related driving incidents and their sleepiness for about 90 minutes compared with a control drink [2]. The study had 12 participants, so it shows a direction and nothing more.

The body clock matters as well. In a lab study where 50 healthy young adults stayed awake for about 54 hours, caffeine's benefit was largest during the early-morning low point, between 6 am and 10 am [3]. The dose was 600 mg, three times EFSA's single-dose figure, so take the principle and leave the number [4]. A 7 am wake-up sits inside that 6 am to 10 am window, which is why the plan below aims a pouch at your first block.

The second half of the problem is interruption. A founder's day is a long run of switches: a pricing question, a hire, an invoice, a customer. Each switch asks you to put one task down and pick up another cleanly. Switching between tasks is the skill the caffeine and L-theanine studies below measured.

Why do coffee, energy drinks and pills only half-fix it?

Coffee works, and if it already gets you through the morning we won't tell you to stop. Two limits are worth knowing.

The first is dose. In a laboratory analysis of 97 shop espressos, caffeine ranged from 25 mg to 214 mg a cup [5]. A US study found a 16 oz brewed coffee averaged 188 mg, and the same drink from the same shop varied between 259 mg and 564 mg on different days [6]. EFSA considers single doses up to 200 mg and habitual intakes up to 400 mg a day from all sources unlikely to raise safety concerns for healthy adults [4]. When your third cup of the day comes from a café, you can't tell how close you are to that ceiling.

The second is tolerance. In 369 caffeine consumers, much of caffeine's apparent boost in regular users turned out to be the reversal of withdrawal, and people who rarely drank caffeine reported more jitteriness [7]. Caffeine did improve reaction speed and tapping in both groups. Regular users can feel tired and foggy within a day of stopping, with symptoms reported after stopping doses as low as 100 mg a day [8]. If you've drunk coffee daily for years, part of what the first cup does is bring you back to baseline.

Energy drinks have their own profile. A 250 ml can of Red Bull has 80 mg of caffeine and 27 g of sugar, according to the manufacturer's UK page [9]. In controlled studies the caffeine did most of the work and the sugar added little [10]. Energy drinks do work after a short night, as the simulator study above shows. We describe NeuroPouch as giving sharper focus than an energy drink. That is our own comparison, and nobody has run a head-to-head trial of a pouch against a can.

Caffeine tablets and powders leave you measuring the dose yourself. In 2024 the UK Food Standards Agency and Food Standards Scotland published guidance on caffeine in food supplements after a death linked to caffeine powder [11]. That guidance isn't about pouches, but it explains why we prefer a fixed unit. A NeuroPouch holds 100 mg, 16 to a tin, and there is nothing to weigh.

Coffee, a can and a tablet each deliver caffeine on its own. The studies in the next section are about caffeine paired with L-theanine.

How do caffeine and L-theanine work in a focus supplement for entrepreneurs who switch tasks all day?

Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, the brain's tiredness signal. A review of its effects on performance found that doses from about 40 mg to 300 mg improve alertness, vigilance, attention and reaction time, while the effects on judgement and decision-making were less consistent [12]. So we don't claim a pouch will make you decide better. It targets alertness and attention.

L-theanine is the amino acid in tea. A 2014 meta-analysis of 11 randomised trials found moderate benefits from the combination of caffeine and L-theanine on alertness and attention switching within two hours of a dose, and the caffeine dose appeared to matter more than the theanine dose [1]. The individual trials used different doses, and the table sets them beside one pouch.

The ratio across these trials ran from 0.8:1 to 2.4:1, and 1.5:1 sits inside that range. No trial tested 150 mg of theanine with 100 mg of caffeine, and we won't pretend one did.

The caveats matter for a founder who will read the abstracts. Every one of these studies was acute, meaning a single dose measured within a couple of hours. Participants were healthy young volunteers. Three of them came from Unilever researchers [14, 15, 16]. In a two-hour vigilance test, the combination was no better than either ingredient alone at 50 mg caffeine and 100 mg theanine, though both beat placebo as errors rose over time [19].

The jitters data cuts against any easy promise. At 250 mg of caffeine, a dose two and a half times a pouch, theanine blunted the rise in blood pressure and did not significantly reduce jitteriness [18]. A later study of 369 caffeine consumers found non-regular consumers reported more jitteriness [7]. It's one more argument for a moderate dose, and 100 mg is half of EFSA's 200 mg single-dose figure.

Testers described the focus as smooth and jitter-free, and that is how we describe it. It is the testers' report and our product description. It isn't a study result.

On speed, most people feel it within about 10 minutes, and Oliver's own account is twenty. Swallowed caffeine peaks in the blood in roughly half an hour [20], and caffeine chewing gum reached peak blood levels faster than a capsule in an 84-man study, though total absorption was similar [21]. We haven't found a published absorption study of caffeine pouches, so what testers feel is experience, not measurement.

What do Alpha-GPC, Rhodiola and B12 add, and what can't they promise?

Alpha-GPC (100 mg). It's a source of choline, which the body uses to make acetylcholine [22]. In a 2024 crossover trial of 20 resistance-trained men, a single 315 mg or 630 mg dose improved scores on one attention test, the Stroop, but not on two others [23]. In a 48-man trial, 250 mg a day for a week left strength and a vigilance test unchanged, though jump power improved [24]. The pouch carries 100 mg, and two pouches reach 200 mg, still below the lowest dose in those trials. In May 2026 EFSA's panel concluded L-alpha-GPC at up to 203.7 mg a day in food supplements raised no safety concerns under the applicant's proposed conditions [22]. That is a safety opinion on an ingredient, and it doesn't approve NeuroPouch. We make no claim that a pouch matches the trial doses.

Rhodiola rosea (75 mg). We describe Rhodiola rosea as reducing mental and physical fatigue. The published research is more mixed than that sentence sounds. In 56 young physicians on night duty, a standardised extract improved scores on a set of mental performance tests compared with placebo, in a study by manufacturer-linked authors [25]. A 2012 systematic review called the evidence contradictory, with all included studies at high or unclear risk of bias [26]. A 2011 review concluded Rhodiola may have beneficial effects but noted the lack of independent replication [27]. The trials used various extracts and doses, so they don't map onto 75 mg. Rhodiola also carries no authorised EU health claim, and a claim about reducing tiredness in stress was refused in 2013 [28].

Vitamin B12 (100 µg, methylcobalamin). Vitamin B12 contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue, and it contributes to normal functioning of the nervous system. That is the EU-authorised wording [29]. It applies to the vitamin, not to the pouch as a whole.

What does a founder's day look like with two pouches?

Use one pouch under the upper lip for 20 to 40 minutes, then bin it. We recommend up to two a day. Two pouches are 200 mg of caffeine, which equals EFSA's single-dose figure and half its daily one [4]. They are spaced hours apart, so you never take the 200 mg at once.

The 14:00 cut-off is our rule of thumb for a bedtime around 23:00, and it isn't a study result. The reasoning has real support. Caffeine usually lengthens the time to fall asleep and shortens sleep, with large differences between people [30]. Its half-life ranged from about 3 to 10 hours across ten healthy men [20]. EFSA notes that 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults, especially close to bedtime [4]. In one at-home study, 400 mg taken even six hours before bed reduced total sleep time [31]. A pouch is a quarter of that dose, so the size of the effect doesn't transfer. The direction does. If a pouch at 4 pm costs you an hour of sleep tonight, you've borrowed from tomorrow's first block.

If you also drink coffee, count it. Two pouches leave 200 mg of EFSA's 400 mg daily figure, which is about two filter coffees at EFSA's typical 90 mg each, spaced out. Café cups often run larger, as the espresso and brewed-coffee figures above show. On a day you've already had strong coffee, skip the second pouch.

On the night you slept four hours, use pouch one as above and move your cut-off earlier. Caffeine hides the tiredness signal for a few hours, and the sleep debt is still there afterwards. Go to bed earlier that night, because nothing in a pouch replaces sleep.

What did Oliver and the first 100 testers report, and what does it prove?

Oliver's account is the one at the top of this page, and more than 100 people tried NeuroPouch before launch and reported pure focus without jitters. Those reports are behind our wording, "smooth, jitter-free focus" and "no crash".

Here is what they can carry. They show that people who tried the pouch described it in consistent terms. They don't show the size of an effect, they can't separate the pouch from expectation, and no trial of the finished product exists. No study measures an energy-drink crash either, so "no crash" is our product description, which the research neither confirms nor contradicts.

Put the three pieces together and each is partial: one founder's mornings, more than 100 testers, and published studies finding better attention switching within two hours in healthy volunteers [1]. None alone settles the question, which is why the first order carries a 30-day refund.

What objections do founders raise?

I'll add it on top of my coffee. Swap rather than stack. Count every source against EFSA's 400 mg a day and keep two pouches inside that.

Will it wreck my sleep, and does it replace sleep? It doesn't replace sleep, and no supplement does. Caffeine hides the signal of tiredness for a few hours, and the debt is still there. Late caffeine can shorten the next night [30], and EFSA notes that even 100 mg may affect sleep in some adults close to bedtime [4]. That's why the plan has a 14:00 cut-off. On a bad night, a pouch gets you into the block, and an early bedtime pays back the debt.

Is it safe, and how much caffeine is too much? We don't use "safe" as a blanket word. EFSA considers single doses up to 200 mg, and up to 400 mg a day from all sources, unlikely to raise safety concerns for healthy adults [4]. Two pouches are 200 mg. How fast you clear caffeine, and how it feels, partly depends on your genes [35]. A 2017 review found caffeine can cause problems in vulnerable groups, including pregnant and breastfeeding women and people with heart conditions [36]. NeuroPouch isn't for under-18s, pregnant or breastfeeding people, or anyone sensitive to caffeine. If you take medication or have a medical condition, ask your doctor first.

Will I get jitters? Testers reported none, and we describe the focus as jitter-free. The studies are less tidy. In one large study, non-regular caffeine consumers reported more jitteriness [7], and theanine did not clearly cut it at a high caffeine dose [18]. If you are sensitive to caffeine, don't use it.

Is the evidence strong? It's modest, and we say so. The caffeine and L-theanine pairing has the best support, in acute studies of healthy volunteers [1]. Rhodiola is mixed, Alpha-GPC is dosed below the trials, and B12 is a labelled vitamin claim. If you want a product with a clinical trial behind the finished formula, none exists for this or for any pouch we know of.

Frequently asked questions

Can caffeine make up for a lack of sleep?

Caffeine can reduce sleepiness for a few hours, but it does not replace sleep. A review found repeated, spaced doses help maintain alertness in jobs with restricted sleep [12]. Late caffeine can also shorten the next night's sleep [30], so it can't pay back the debt. An early bedtime does that.

How much caffeine is too much in a day?

EFSA considers single doses up to 200 mg, and habitual intakes up to 400 mg a day from all sources, unlikely to raise safety concerns for healthy adults, and up to 200 mg a day for pregnant women [4]. Two NeuroPouch pouches are 200 mg. Café coffee varies widely, so count every source.

Does L-theanine with caffeine really help you focus?

Studies on the pairing found better attention switching and alertness in healthy volunteers within two hours of a single dose [1]. They were small and acute, some were industry-funded, and none tested NeuroPouch itself. Read more in our L-theanine and caffeine guide.

How quickly does a NeuroPouch work, and how long does it last?

Most people feel it within about 10 minutes, and Oliver's account is about twenty. Use one pouch for 20 to 40 minutes. Caffeine's half-life varied from about 3 to 10 hours between individuals in one small study [20], so the effect fades gradually rather than at a fixed time.

Can I use NeuroPouch if I drink coffee?

Yes, if you count the coffee. Keep total caffeine under 400 mg a day from all sources. Café coffee varies widely, from 25 mg to over 200 mg an espresso in one analysis [5], so swap a coffee for a pouch rather than stacking one on top.
